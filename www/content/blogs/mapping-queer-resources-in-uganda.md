# Mapping queer resources in Uganda, one contributor at a time

Transspace started from a personal problem: while navigating a transition in Uganda, reliable information about healthcare providers, safe spaces, and legal support existed, but only as word of mouth, scattered across private conversations and group chats that anyone new to the community had no way to find.

## Choosing the first categories

Rather than guess at a taxonomy, the first categories came from asking people directly what they'd searched for and failed to find:

1. Healthcare and transition support
2. Friendly spaces, businesses and venues
3. Jobs and mentorship
4. Legal and education support
5. Mutual aid

That list will keep changing. It's a record of what people actually needed when we asked, not a fixed structure to defend later.

## What surprised us

The knowledge people were willing to share was almost always local and specific, a named clinic, a named landlord, a named person to talk to, rather than general advice. General advice was already everywhere. What was missing was the specific, verified, current version of it. That's the gap Transspace is actually trying to close.

## Where this goes next

Local knowledge, shared with people facing the same situation somewhere else. Someone in Kenya benefiting from something first documented in Uganda. That's the whole mechanism, and it only works if the first round of categories are honest about what people actually asked for.
